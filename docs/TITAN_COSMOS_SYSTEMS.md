# TITAN COSMOS — ten mathematical / physics-inspired research systems

The names are **metaphors**, not supernatural or divine capabilities; all calculations use completed public OKX candles and order-book rows.

1. Quantum amplitude interference proxy — signed-return / absolute-return ratio, not quantum hardware.
2. Ising spin alignment — sign magnetization and nearest-neighbor return coupling.
3. Shannon entropy — three-state price-change disorder.
4. Fourier spectral resonance — top-three DFT-bin concentration.
5. Hurst diffusion exponent proxy — one- vs four-lag log-price increment variances.
6. Ornstein–Uhlenbeck reversion proxy — log-price z score and AR(1) half life.
7. Extreme-tail event horizon — 95th percentile versus median log-return shock magnitude.
8. Escape velocity momentum — 12-candle return sum divided by realized volatility.
9. Gravitational orderbook pressure — signed bid/ask depth imbalance (first 20 levels).
10. Multiscale phase coherence — 15m/1h/4h directional alignment.

Every system emits deterministic `DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY` numeric evidence. The scanner stores `cosmos_math` in each market's analysis report and `cosmos_system_count=10` in its summary. **No system can create orders, change leverage, or override any existing LONG/SHORT/WAIT, Omega, exchange, risk, or TP/SL safety checks.** No predictive power or profitability has been established.

The single-position `full_margin` mode has a separate planned equity-at-stop budget of **20%**, not a maximum possible realized loss. It is very high risk. Its stop can fail through gaps, price jumps, slippage, liquidation or other market mechanics. The one-position mode can allocate up to 100% of free isolated margin, subject to exchange maximum size, tick size, and veto checks; this can quickly liquidate an account.

Owner stop flags remain set to `OKX_STOP_CONTINUOUS=YES`, `OKX_ENABLE_REAL_TRADING=NO` during development. Do not interpret research-only diagnostics or passing unit tests as evidence the trading strategy has an out-of-sample advantage.
